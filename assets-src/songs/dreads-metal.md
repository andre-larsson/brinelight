- A Dread fight often ends before a three-minute song does, so 1 to 4 ask to be heavy within the first ten seconds. The
  Cantor's fight is a long final, so its song may open slowly.
- After the Buoy refusal ("themes that might come across as potentially sensitive or dark"), the words keep the threat
  to creature-feature level: no blood, no killing, and death only as "the keepers who stayed". The style words "dark"
  and "heavy" went through in the Buoy's versions B and D.
- Each song is one voice (a male voice here, for contrast with the women's voices at the Buoy and on the title screen),
  with gang shouts or a choir where asked.

## 5. Ring It Once Again (the Drowned Cantor; Iron Maiden-like epic)

than the last, Call the Choir. From WORLD.md: the keepers once rang the bell by hand with a rope up the tower; the

keeps the keepers who stay with it; it is not a fact yet.

```

The choir answers the singer in every chorus like a congregation. In the instrumental the bell rings faster and louder on every beat. At the end the band stops, the choir fades out one voice at a time, and the bell rings once more, quietly. Grand, solemn, heroic and sad. About 3 minutes.
