# The Tolling Buoy's song: the singer in the cage (request for Gemini, v0.36.0)

Pending: the user generates it. Until it exists, the Buoy's fight plays the music-box cue `buoy` ("The Rocking Toll" in
the Jukebox). It is in A minor, 6/8, at 52, so every version here is in the same key and time.

Who sings it: the singer in the cage (WORLD.md, "the singer in the cage"). The light-eaters in the Buoy's cage have
grown into the shape of a woman kneeling with her arms round the bell. She sings what the bell rings. She never leaves
the cage. Whose shape it is stays open; the words only hint that it is the shape of someone the bell once rang for.
They name nothing, and say nothing about what is below Dark Water.

**Gentle versions (2026-11-16).** Gemini refused the first version A: "steering clear of generating music around
themes that might come across as potentially sensitive or dark". So every version here keeps the mood (wistful,
mysterious, a bell on a chain, a big peal in the bridge) and leaves out death, drowning and danger: no "dark", "cold",
"sank", "lost", "drowning", "cry" or "haunting", no boat that did not make it, and no one lured down. The first wordings
are in git history (commit 05df1f3). If a version is still refused, try E, which has no words.

## A: atmospheric folk sea waltz

```
A slow, wistful sea waltz in 6/8, about 52 BPM, in A minor. One female voice, soft, warm and close, like a lullaby, with long gliding notes. Under her: the gentle creak of a rocking chain; an old bell-buoy's bell that chimes on each swing, a little unsteady, as if the waves rock it; a low cello and a bowed double bass holding a long note on A; slow waves far off; now and then a huge bell, very far away. Spacious, with long reverb, like a great sea cave lit by glowing water. The bridge builds into a grand peal: the bell on every beat, timpani, swelling gongs and low brass, and her voice soaring high. Then a great wave rolls over everything, and the gentle rocking returns for the last chorus. Atmospheric folk, ethereal, cinematic, mysterious and bittersweet. No drum kit, no guitar, no synth pads. About 3 minutes.

Lyrics:

[Intro]
(humming, no words)

[Verse 1]
Off the harbour rocks I swung on my chain,
I rang so the boats would hear,
I rang through the fog and I rang through the rain,
and I rang for the one who was late that year.

[Chorus]
Hush now, hush, the tide is turning,
rest on the bronze, the water's clear,
I only sing what the bell is saying,
deep in the blue where the lights appear.

[Verse 2]
Then the wind came up and the chain swung free,
and I settled down where the deep tides go,
and the little lights came to sing with me,
and they took the shape of someone I know.

[Chorus]
Hush now, hush, the tide is turning,
rest on the bronze, the water's clear,
I only sing what the bell is saying,
deep in the blue where the lights appear.

[Bridge]
Swing, the chain is singing,
swing, the tide is calling,
ring it out, ring it out,
ring till the water's falling.

[Final Chorus]
(quiet again, after the wave)
Hush now, hush, the tide is turning,
a lantern is coming down the stair,
I only sing what the bell is saying,
and the bell says: she is nearly there.

[Outro]
(humming, one last bell, the gentle creak of the chain)
```

## C: Nordic folk song with kulning, a counting rhyme

A buoy's count of what it rang for, kept like a herding song. The hint: she holds the bell "as someone held on". The last
verse says she lets go when Wren reaches the bottom step, which is what happens when the Buoy lies still.

```
A Nordic folk song in 6/8, about 52 BPM, in A minor. One female voice, clear and plain like a lullaby in the verses, and between verses high, ringing kulning calls (the Scandinavian herding call) that echo off stone. A hurdy-gurdy drone on A and E, a nyckelharpa answering her lines, a slow frame drum like a heartbeat, an iron bell struck on each swing of a rocking buoy, the creak of a chain and the sea against rock. Spacious and airy, as if recorded in a great sea cave. In the bridge the frame drums multiply, the hurdy-gurdy grows strong, the bell rings on every beat, and her kulning soars; then a great wave rolls over it all, and the last verse is almost whispered over the drone alone. Old and acoustic: no guitar, no synths, no modern drums. About 3 minutes.

Lyrics:

[Intro]
(kulning, high wordless calls, the bell on the swing)

[Verse 1]
One for the rocks and two for the reef,
three for the fog that hides them,
I rang them all from the harbour mouth,
and the boats came home beside them.

[Chorus]
Sing low, sing low what the bell rings,
sing low to the bronze and the water,
the last I rang for was late coming in,
and I hold the bell as someone held on.

[Verse 2]
Four for the wind and five for the chain,
six for the deep that kept me,

and the shape they made to hold me.

[Chorus]
Sing low, sing low what the bell rings,
sing low to the bronze and the water,
the last I rang for was late coming in,
and I hold the bell as someone held on.

[Final Verse]
(whispered, over the drone)
One for the rocks and two for the reef,
three for the light on the stair,

and let go when you get there.

[Outro]
(one last kulning call, far away; the bell; the sea)
```
