# The Tolling Buoy's song: the singer in the cage (request for Gemini, v0.36.0)

Pending: the user generates it. Until it exists, the Buoy's fight plays the music-box cue `buoy` ("The Rocking Toll" in
the Jukebox). It is in A minor, 6/8, at 52, so every version here is in the same key and time.

Who sings it: the singer in the cage (WORLD.md, "the singer in the cage"). The light-eaters in the Buoy's cage have
grown into the shape of a woman kneeling with her arms round the bell. She sings what the bell rings. She never leaves
the cage. Whose shape it is stays open; the words only hint that it is the shape of someone the bell once rang for.
They name nothing, and say nothing about what is below Dark Water.

**Only version A was refused (2026-11-16).** Gemini: "steering clear of generating music around themes that might come
across as potentially sensitive or dark". B, C and D went through as they are below. A here is a gentler rewording: the
same mood (wistful, mysterious, a bell on a chain, a big peal in the bridge) with no death, drowning or danger in it (no
"dark", "cold", "sank", "haunting" or "cry", and no boat that did not make it). The refused wording is in git history
(commit 05df1f3). If A is refused again, try E, which has no words.

## A: atmospheric folk sea waltz (reworded after the refusal)

```
A slow, wistful sea waltz in 6/8, about 52 BPM, in A minor. One female voice, soft, warm and close, like a lullaby, with long gliding notes. Under her: the gentle creak of a rocking chain; an old bell-buoy's bell that chimes on each swing, a little unsteady, as if the waves rock it; a low cello and a bowed double bass holding a long note on A; slow waves far off; now and then a huge bell, very far away. Spacious, with long reverb, like a great sea cave lit by glowing water. The bridge builds into a grand peal: the bell on every beat, timpani, swelling gongs and low brass, and her voice soaring high. Then a great wave rolls over everything, and the gentle rocking returns for the last chorus. Atmospheric folk, ethereal, cinematic, mysterious and bittersweet. No drum kit, no guitar, no synth pads. About 3 minutes.

Lyrics:

[Intro]
(humming, no words)

[Verse 1]
Off the harbour rocks I swung on my chain,
I rang so the boats would hear,
I rang through the fog and I rang through the rain,
and I rang for the one who was late that year.

[Chorus]
Hush now, hush, the tide is turning,
rest on the bronze, the water's clear,
I only sing what the bell is saying,
deep in the blue where the lights appear.

[Verse 2]
Then the wind came up and the chain swung free,
and I settled down where the deep tides go,
and the little lights came to sing with me,
and they took the shape of someone I know.

[Chorus]
Hush now, hush, the tide is turning,
rest on the bronze, the water's clear,
I only sing what the bell is saying,
deep in the blue where the lights appear.

[Bridge]
Swing, the chain is singing,
swing, the tide is calling,
ring it out, ring it out,
ring till the water's falling.

[Final Chorus]
(quiet again, after the wave)
Hush now, hush, the tide is turning,
a lantern is coming down the stair,
I only sing what the bell is saying,
and the bell says: she is nearly there.

[Outro]
(humming, one last bell, the gentle creak of the chain)
```

## B: darkwave, trip-hop pulse, she sings to the light on the stair

She speaks to Wren's lantern coming down. The buoy was the warning off the rocks; now it is the rock and the warning
both, so she warns and calls at once. The hint: the lights "wore the face they found that day".

```
A dark, slow trip-hop and darkwave song in 6/8, about 52 BPM, in A minor. A deep sub-bass pulse that pulls and lets go like a chain on a swell, a slow heavy half-time beat with a soft, wet snare, and a metallic bell sample that rings on each swing, detuned and drowned in reverb. One female voice, low and breathy, close to the microphone, doubled by her own whisper an octave down; on the chorus her voice opens up, high and aching. Reverse-reverb swells, the hiss of the sea, a far-off great bell very low. The bridge is a storm: the beat doubles, distorted bells crash on every beat, the bass growls, and her voice turns into a cry over it; then the roar of a breaking wave sweeps everything away, and only the pulse and her whisper are left. Cinematic, cold, hypnotic. No guitar, no bright synth leads, no rap. About 3 minutes.

Lyrics:

[Intro]
(whispered) Come down... come down...

[Verse 1]
I was the warning off the harbour rocks,
I rang the boats away from harm,
now I am the rock and the warning both,
with the cold bell held in my arms.

[Pre-Chorus]
Little light, little light on the stair,
can you hear what the bell is saying?

[Chorus]
Turn back, turn back, the water's deep,
the bell is ringing what it cannot keep,
turn back, turn back, or come to me,
I will sing you down where the boats used to be.

[Verse 2]
I waited a hundred years for one,

[Pre-Chorus]
Little light, little light on the stair,
can you hear what the bell is saying?

[Chorus]
Turn back, turn back, the water's deep,

[Bridge]

ring it wild, ring it wild,
swing high, swing low, the chain is gone,

[Final Chorus]
(after the wave, only the pulse and a whisper)
Turn back, turn back... or come to me,

[Outro]
(whispered) Come down.
```

## C: Nordic folk lament with kulning, a counting rhyme

A buoy's count of what it warned of, kept like a herding song. The hint: she holds the bell "as someone held on", the
way a drowning sailor holds a buoy in a storm. The last verse says she lets go when Wren reaches the bottom step, which
is what happens when the Buoy lies still.

```
A Nordic folk lament in 6/8, about 52 BPM, in A minor. One female voice, clear and plain like a lullaby in the verses, and between verses high, ringing kulning calls (the Scandinavian herding call) that echo off stone. A hurdy-gurdy drone on A and E, a nyckelharpa answering her lines, a slow frame drum like a heartbeat, an iron bell struck on each swing of a rocking buoy, the creak of a chain and the sea against rock. Stark, cold and spacious, as if recorded in a sea cave. In the bridge the frame drums multiply, the hurdy-gurdy grinds hard, the bell rings on every beat, and her kulning becomes a wild cry; then a wave crashes over it all, and the last verse is almost whispered over the drone alone. Old and acoustic: no guitar, no synths, no modern drums. About 3 minutes.

Lyrics:

[Intro]
(kulning, high wordless calls, the bell on the swing)

[Verse 1]
One for the rocks and two for the reef,
three for the fog that hides them,
I rang them all from the harbour mouth,
and the boats came home beside them.

[Chorus]
Sing low, sing low what the bell rings,
sing low to the bronze and the water,
the last I rang for never came in,
and I hold the bell as someone held on.

[Verse 2]
Four for the storm and five for the chain,
six for the dark that took me,
seven for the lights that came through the bars,
and the shape they made to hold me.

[Chorus]
Sing low, sing low what the bell rings,
sing low to the bronze and the water,
the last I rang for never came in,
and I hold the bell as someone held on.

[Bridge]
Eight, and the chain is broken,
nine, and the bell rings wild,
ten, and the water's falling,
(kulning cry)

[Final Verse]
(whispered, over the drone)
One for the rocks and two for the reef,
three for the light on the stair,
I will ring you down to the bottom step,
and let go when you get there.

[Outro]
(one last kulning call, far away; the bell; the sea)
```

Above them, wide ambient layers: clean guitar harmonics that ring like small bells, shimmering delay, an old bell-buoy's iron bell that strikes on each swing, the sea in the background.
