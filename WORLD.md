# Brinelight world

This file describes the target setting and story for Brinelight. Future game design, cards, events, and screens should follow the agreed details here. Some of them are not in the game yet. Rules and balance are in README.md and GAME_NOTES.md. Card and enemy lists are in js/data.js.

Add a detail here when future game text will need to stay consistent with it. When a screen changes, update the relevant detail in the same commit. Each detail has a status:

- **In the game:** Already stated or shown in the current game, or in the README where noted. These details document the starting point and can differ from the target.
- **Agreed:** A decision for the target world. Where it differs from the current game, the game needs to change to match it.
- **Open:** An unanswered question. When it is answered, record the decision and remove the question from the open list.

On 2026-11-12 the user handed the world-building and lore to the agent. Decisions from that day are marked Agreed like the others, and the user can overrule any of them.

The code and saved games keep some older names: `tide` for the Swell, `low` and `lowWater` for a Strand, `charge` for Roil, and `floor` for the landing.

## The idea

The keeper's craft has two halves: the light and the bell. Wren plays with both. Light is what she spends on cards, and her bell moves the water. The story is what happens when the great bell takes the great light.

### Environmental theme and cosmic horror (2026-11-12; agreed, not built)

- **Agreed:** The underlying story is that the keepers changed the Reach to make Saltmere safer, and something has grown in the conditions they created. This is the central cause; other environmental ideas should support it without becoming several separate explanations for the crisis.
- **Agreed:** The magical coast is a metaphor for human impact on the environment. Human progress can save lives and support livelihoods while also causing unintended, accumulating harm. The lighthouse and the keepers' tools have real benefits. Responsibility can differ between people who could not foresee the effects and people who later understood them.
- **Agreed:** Keep the environmental meaning underneath the adventure. Show changes through places, creatures, objects and keeper records. The opening still gives Wren a concrete task: the light is gone, the water is rising, and she must bring the Heart home. Avoid moral speeches and a simple division into good nature and bad humans.
- **Agreed:** Cosmic horror comes from the scale and nature of what has taken hold around human work. Wren can learn how to handle particular dangers while remaining unsure what the larger thing is or what it perceives. Human activity has consequences even when the thing responding has no human intention to punish anyone.
- **Agreed:** The ending is ambiguous about what survives and what the Reach is becoming. Wren's immediate success is clear: she recovers the Heart and saves Saltmere. Useful knowledge, better equipment and adaptation can improve life afterward, even where changes to the coast last.
- **Open:** The nature of what has grown around the bell, and how much Wren can discover about it. The exact final scene is also open. A faint light remaining in the Belfry after the Heart is removed is a candidate image, not a fixed ending.

## The situation

Wren is the last lighthouse keeper of Saltmere. The lighthouse went dark on her watch. Far below, the Drowned Bellwether rang with no hand on its rope, and the Lantern Heart went down to it. Wren goes down the coast to bring it back.

The current game (v0.19.0) tells this story in a shorter shape: an expedition of two levels. The Drowned Bellwether is the Shallows' pin. When it breaks, the Heart is not in it: its glow goes on down the drained stair. Wren can go home there, or go deeper into Dark Water, where the Tolling Buoy holds the Heart in its cage. Breaking the Buoy brings the Heart home. This is a stopgap: the agreed story puts the Bellwether and the Heart in the Belfry, below a second level, and gives the Shallows a pin of its own (slice B).

- **In the game:** The title screen says an enchanted bell, sunk under the cliffs, has stolen the fire from your lighthouse and is pulling the sea up toward the town. You are the last keeper, and you go down to take the fire back.
- **In the game:** An opening in four pictures plays before the first descent. It says who Wren is, what the Lantern Heart and the Bellwether are, that the bell rang by itself tonight and pulled the Heart down, and what Wren carries. Its text is in `BL.STORY.opening` in js/data.js. Since v0.23.0 its first three pictures are its own: the lighthouse burning over Saltmere's harbour, the Bellwether hanging untended in its drowned chamber (no fire yet), and the night the fire was torn out of the lamp room and pulled down the cliff into a sea cave.
- **In the game:** The README says the keepers' great bell rang with no hand on its rope, and that Wren goes down through the Drowned Reach to take the Lantern Heart back from the Drowned Bellwether.
- **In the game (v0.20.0):** The Shallows' pin is the Harbour Hermit. When its harbour bell cracks, the crab lets go of the water, and the water runs away down a stair that has been under the sea for a hundred years. The Heart is not there. Far below something is still ringing, and the fire's glow goes on down the stair toward it. (In v0.19.0 the Bellwether stood there and said the same.) Going home from a pin that is not the last, the screen is titled "Home with your haul": the bells start again behind her and the water creeps back over the landings.
- **In the game (v0.20.0):** When the Tolling Buoy breaks, its water runs down the last stair, and at the bottom it does not drain: below Dark Water is the Belfry, whose floor is under the sea. (From v0.15.0 to v0.19.0 the Heart was caught in the Buoy's cage, and that ended the expedition.)
- **In the game (v0.20.0):** The expedition ends in the Belfry. When the Bellwether's bronze breaks, the Heart's light pours out through the water, and the thing that grew into the bell comes out with it. When the thing lets go of the Heart, it sinks away into the dark under the floor. Wren carries the Heart up three levels of stairs, and by dawn the great light is turning again. The screen is titled "The Lantern Heart rises" and its button says "Go down again".
- **In the game:** On a loss, the cold water closes over Wren's lantern and she goes under. The sea carries her back to shore, alive. The lighthouse is still dark, and the water has risen a little higher toward the town. The screen is titled "The tide takes you" and its button says "Try again". Once the Heart is home (v0.23.0), a fall or a homecoming says instead that the great light still turns over the town, and a homecoming shows it lit.
- **Agreed:** Each boss above the final depth holds water in its own area, covering a stair or tunnel. Defeating the boss drains the area and opens the passage down. The Heart remains deeper down, and the lighthouse is restored by dawn only after Wren clears the final drowned depth.
- **Agreed:** Wren does not die when she falls. Her lantern burns down to an ember but stays alight, because it holds the last of the Heart's fire. The held water takes her. Past the bell's reach the ordinary sea still gives back what it takes, and it returns her to the Shallows alive.
- **Open:** What Wren loses when she falls, besides the keepsakes she found on that expedition.

## The light and the bell

- **Agreed:** The Lantern Heart is the fire of Saltmere's great light. Every keeper's lantern is lit from it. The Light that Wren spends on cards is the same fire.
- **Agreed:** A lantern can be lit from another keeper's lantern, and that costs the giver something. Wren's lantern is the last flame lit from the Heart.
- **In the game:** Sharing her light with the Lost Lamplighter costs Wren 6 HP.
- **Agreed:** The Drowned Bellwether is the keepers' own great bell. Every keeper's hand-bell is tuned to it. It leads and they follow, which is what a bellwether is.
- **Agreed:** Keepers sank the Bellwether under the Reach long ago, to hold the sea off the low stairs. They walked down at the lowest spring tides to tend it.
- **Agreed:** A hundred years ago the Lost Lamplighter drowned on the low stair. He was the last keeper to tend the bell. Nobody went down after him.
- **Agreed:** Nobody rang the bell. The tide swings its clapper and the bell holds the tide, so it cannot stop on its own. It has rung a little louder every year since it was last tended.
- **Agreed:** The keepers cast the Bellwether themselves, as a hand-bell big enough to move the sea.
- **Agreed:** The Heart followed the bell down. A keeper's light answers the lead bell, as a hand-bell does.
- **Agreed:** Brinelight is the Heart's light soaked into the held water. It is the glow in the Glimmering Pool, the bottle, the sea chest, and the pearls.
- **In the game:** Saltmere's great light can go out. Bringing the Lantern Heart up can set it turning again by dawn.
- **In the game (v0.20.0):** The Lantern Heart still burns, under water as well as in air. It lay in the Belfry under the Bellwether, held by the thing that grew into the bell. When the bronze breaks, its light heals Wren (30% of her Max HP) and burns beside her for the rest of the fight: Heartfire, 1 more Light each turn.
- **Open:** What the forgotten sea goddess has to do with the bell and the Heart. Her stone hands are held out and empty.

## Why they go down

- **Agreed:** Wren goes because the light went out on her watch, and because hers is the last flame lit from the Heart.
- **Agreed:** Saltmere needs her to go. The ordinary sea gives back what it takes. Held water gives nothing back, so the wrecks, the drowned, and the Heart stay down. Each spring tide the held water stands a stair closer to the town. The game text says it more simply: the bell lifts the sea toward the town, a little higher with every tide. The rules have no countdown for this.
- **Agreed:** Before the Heart comes home, everything Wren opens closes behind her because the Bellwether restores the water after each expedition. Reaching the final depth and silencing it ends the immediate threat to Saltmere. The altered Reach remains after that victory; see "After the Heart comes home" below.
- **Agreed:** Carrying a lantern lit from the Heart is what makes a keeper. Wren is the last keeper when the game starts. Later playable characters become keepers when she lights their lanterns from hers. This is how the README's "last lighthouse keeper" and the loss screen's "keepers" fit together.
- **Agreed:** Each later keeper has something of their own under the same bell.
- **Open:** Who the later keepers are. Three ideas: a salvor after the Gilded Gull's cargo, a fisher whose partner is among the Drowned Deckhands, and the Tide-Witch's apprentice, who wants the bell and not the Heart.

### After the Heart comes home (2026-11-12; agreed direction, not built)

- **Agreed:** The game should support indefinitely repeated expeditions in the same continuing world. The first full victory changes Wren's reason for returning: recovering the Heart gives way to dealing with the consequences of generations of change to the coast.
- **Agreed:** The Heart stays home and the lighthouse stays lit. The Bellwether remains silenced; later runs do not undo that victory by repeatedly stealing the Heart or restoring the same bell. The story continues through disturbances in the altered Reach, alongside salvage and discoveries.
- **Agreed:** Later expeditions should still have achievable ends. Local successes matter and can last while other disturbances arise. The ongoing struggle leaves room for people to improve their lives and adapt.
- **Agreed:** Keep the descent structure usable after the ending: begin in the Shallows, cross held chambers, gather things, and choose whether to return or continue. Held water and threats can recur after the great bell falls silent. This supersedes the earlier idea that silencing it permanently ends all held water in the Reach.
- **In the game (v0.20.0):** After the Heart is home, the Belfry's last landing holds one of two finals. One is the Thing in the Bell, grown back in the broken bronze with its arms. The other is the Drowned Cantor, who leads a choir of drowned keepers trying to ring the bell again. Beating either quiets the Belfry: "The Belfry goes quiet. The broken bell hangs still over the place where the Heart lay, and a faint light stays behind there, as it always does." The chapter *What stays below* says things gather around that light. More finals can be added.
- **Open:** What restores the water and the ringers after the Bellwether is silenced, beyond what *What stays below* says. Their behaviour must support repeated play, but the larger cause can remain uncertain. Things gathering around the broken bell are a possibility, not a chosen set of enemies.
- **Open:** The existing explanations for fresh keepsakes being called back and Bell Minnows following Roil depend on the Bellwether. How those effects continue after its defeat needs to be settled alongside the later expeditions.

## The coast and the tides

Saltmere stands on a headland beside a working sea. The Drowned Reach is a series of coastal rock terraces, or bands. These are above the outside sea level, not holes in the seabed. At the start of the story, the Bellwether is holding seawater in the inner chambers.

- **Agreed:** The lighthouse warns ships away from the rocks. It can do that whether or not the Reach is dry. Ordinary low tide uncovers the Shallows: rock, pools, and the sea below the cliff.
- **In the game (v0.23.0):** The Shallows' fight picture is that foreshore at night: wet rock shelves, pools and kelp, the keepers' first stair cut into the cliff, and the lighthouse dark on the headland. The Harbour Hermit's lock is a rock basin under the cliff, held brim-full and still above the tide, with an old stair going down into it. (Until v0.22.0 both pictures were under water, left over from an older version of the game in which Wren swam.)
- **Agreed:** Below the Shallows, the coast has been wet for a long time. Its old stairs, quay, wrecks, and cold-shore animals existed before the current ringing and its pins. The keepers built the stairs and the quay. The Lost Lamplighter's cold lantern belongs to that older coast.
- **Agreed:** Each shallower band boss is a "pin", a ringer that carries out the Bellwether's work before the Heart is recovered. Each pin has a bell of its own. It holds seawater in its own area, above the level reached by the ordinary tide. The water covers a stair or tunnel leading deeper. Keepers once walked these routes during the lowest spring tides. Those opportunities no longer occur.
- **Agreed:** A held chamber can be called a lock. A pin holds a lock shut, and breaking the pin opens it.
- **Agreed:** Wren reaches the boss by walking and climbing along exposed paths, stairs, and ledges. She fights from stone while water surrounds parts of the area. She carries an open-flame lantern, so she cannot use the submerged passage. Waiting on shore uncovers only the Shallows.
- **Agreed:** When Wren defeats the boss, it stops holding the water. The water drains from its area and exposes the passage down. Ordinary high tide still wets the Shallows but does not reach the inner lip. The drained area remains wet, with pools and shallow water.
- **Agreed:** The water held in a chamber does not move because of an ordinary fight. It drains when the pin goes silent.
- **Agreed:** Wren can always return home safely after defeating a boss, both before and after recovering the Heart. While the Bellwether still rings, it starts drawing water back as she retreats, but her route home remains safe.
- **Agreed:** Returning to the lighthouse ends the expedition. Wren unloads her haul, rests, and prepares before setting out again. While the Bellwether still rings, it has by then restored the water in the opened areas. It calls back a surviving boss or draws a similar creature to replace it. The boss or its replacement holds water in the area again, and other sea creatures return with the water. Wren cannot make a quick visit home and resume the same expedition. The source of the later recurrence remains open, as described above.
- **Agreed:** The final chamber is the Belfry. It has no lip above the sea. Its floor is below the waterline, and its opening leads to the sea. Before the first full victory, water is held around the Heart there. Wren enters in the diving suit, and the chamber does not drain.
- **Open:** Who the pins are. Three ideas: a giant hermit crab wearing a harbour bell, a rusted bell-buoy on its chain, and a drowned ringer.

### The Swell in a fight

The coast has the tide. A fight has the Swell. The two are kept apart so that Wren's local water-working is not confused with the tide that covers the bands.

- **In the game:** The fight words are Swell, Surge, Ebb, Crest, and Strand. The Swell is the gauge. Surge raises it and Ebb lowers it. It Crests at 6 and Strands the enemies at 0.
- **Agreed:** The Swell is the water around the enemies, moved with the Brass Tide-Bell. Wren stands on stone, and enemies are in the wash.
- **Agreed:** Surge builds water onto the enemies. A Crest is a wave breaking on them. After it breaks, the water falls back to slack, represented by the gauge resetting to the middle. Another Surge in the same turn raises a new wave from that slack, and it breaks harder.
- **Agreed:** Ebb pulls water off the enemies. A Strand leaves them stranded and removes their cover. The opening gives Wren 1 Light. The surrounding sea then seeps back to slack.
- **Agreed:** The Hollow Siren flattens the Swell so a wave cannot build. The Bellwether can pull the water in the other direction. If it pulls the water all the way out, whoever is in it is stranded.
- **Agreed:** A Crest at the top of the gauge is a wave Wren has raised. The sea takes her when she falls, not when the gauge Crests.
- **In the game:** Two or more Crests in a turn earn the Tidal Fury bonus. Two or more Strands in a turn earn High and Dry.
- **Agreed:** Some names keep the word "tide": the Brass Tide-Bell, Tide Reading, Spring Tide, Tidal Memory, Tidewatch, Tidal Fury, and Slack Water. Keepers call a hand-bell a tide-bell because it is tuned to the bell that holds the tide.

## The expedition

Every expedition starts on shore in the Shallows and can continue through every band Wren is willing to risk. Wren packs once on shore and carries the same pack throughout. Starting a new expedition at a deeper band is not possible.

### Initial three-level scope (2026-11-12; agreed, built in v0.20.0)

Plan for three levels initially. More intermediary bands can be added later. These are physical stages of one expedition. Since v0.20.0 the game has all three: the Shallows, Dark Water and the Belfry.

| Level | Story role | Progression |
|---|---|---|
| First: the Shallows | Introduce the coast, the keepers' work and signs of change. | Its boss opens the route to the second level. |
| Second: Dark Water | Explore more of the altered Reach and the consequences of the bell. This is the last walkable band. | Clearing its boss for the first time gives Wren the diving suit. |
| Final: the Belfry | Confront the Bellwether and recover the Heart on the first full victory. | Entry requires the suit brought from shore. Later expeditions end with one of the Belfry's finals (v0.20.0). |

The suit still has to be carried home before it can be worn on a later expedition. Every attempt begins in the Shallows. If Wren packs the suit, it occupies a worn slot from the start. Earlier concepts for Dark Water, Riptide and the Abyss are retained below for possible later use; they are not three additional levels in this initial scope.

### Expedition rules

- **Agreed:** After defeating a band boss, Wren can always return home safely or continue through the newly opened passage.
- **Agreed:** Returning home ends the expedition. Wren keeps everything collected on that expedition, but depth progress resets. The next expedition begins in the Shallows.
- **Agreed:** Continuing is the only way to reach richer finds and make progress farther down in that expedition. The expedition also ends if Wren completes its final objective or falls. The first full completion recovers the Heart; later completions belong to the continuing struggle described above.
- **Agreed:** Keepsakes are kept between expeditions, as the name says. If Wren falls, she loses the keepsakes she found on that expedition. She keeps the ones she brought from home.
- **Agreed:** A keepsake fresh from the Reach still answers the Bellwether, so the bell calls it back when Wren falls. A night in the Lazaret makes a keepsake hers.
- **Agreed:** A keepsake that has spent a night in the Lazaret answers its keeper. If Wren leaves it behind in the caves, it is in the Lazaret again when she gets home. Nobody has seen one travel.
- **Agreed (v0.13.0): what a Roiled aether does, in order.** First it sheds seawater into Wren's pack: Seep. Stirred further, the aether thrashes like a hooked fish and strikes her at the start of every turn: Thrash. It has no aim, it only hits whoever is nearest. It is a blow, so a guard stops it: a keeper who is still braced when it comes takes nothing, and the price is the Ward she held back for it. (Changed in v0.14.0. In v0.13.0 this was called Scour, it built up over a few turns, and nothing stopped it.) Further still, the noise carries. The Bellwether listens for Roil, and sends a Bell Minnow to find what is calling. The minnow that arrives is a small one. Then the ladder goes round again.
- **In the game (v0.13.0):** The Lazaret opens after the first win. A win keeps every keepsake Wren carries. Before a descent she chooses any number to bring, and each counts toward the Hold. A fall loses only the keepsakes found on that descent. The Lazaret holds one of each, and a second one becomes Salvage: the keepers break it up for its brass and bone.
- **Open:** How the rest of what Wren brings home is kept or used between expeditions.
- **Agreed:** The pack does not grow and cannot be repacked after breaking a pin. If Wren brings the diving suit, it occupies a worn slot from the first band onward and has no effect in fights. Carrying it therefore makes every band above the last harder. She cannot enter the Belfry without it.
- **Agreed:** Wren finds the suit the first time she clears the last walkable band, initially the second level. She cannot put it on during that expedition, so she has to carry it home. From then on she can pack it on shore. It is an old keeper's diving dress with a sealed lamp. Her open lantern establishes that the bands above the Belfry contain air.
- **In the game (v0.22.0):** Once the Heart is home, the Workshop can make the Plated Dress: the same dress mended again, with shell plates sewn over the chest. It opens the Belfry like the first, and the plates give 3 Ward at the start of each fight. The mended Diving Dress stays the plain one.
- **In the game (v0.20.0):** The Diving Dress hangs above the Belfry's stair the first time Wren breaks Dark Water's pin without wearing one. Its seams have rotted and its lamp is dry, so she carries it home and mends it. From then on it is in the Workshop's Coat place (not for sale), and wearing it means leaving the Cork Vest or the Shell Gorget at home. It does nothing in a fight. In the Belfry, Wren's picture wears it. A debug-cheated run carries nothing home, the dress included.
- **In the game:** The stops down the map are landings. The top bar reads "Landing 3/8" (8 a level since v0.19.0), and the pin waits on the last landing. A level is one band of an expedition: the Shallows, Dark Water, then the Belfry (v0.20.0).
- **In the game:** Going down is a descent, and Wren sets out. The game says "dive" only for the flooded hold of the Gilded Gull.

## People and creatures

- **In the game:** Wren, the Keeper, is the last lighthouse keeper. She wears an oilskin, a scarf, and boots, and carries a harpoon and an open lantern. In the Belfry she wears the old keeper's diving dress over them (v0.20.0).
- **In the game (v0.24.0, redrawn in v0.24.1):** Wren's picture follows her coat. The Cork Vest is strapped over her oilskin, and the Shell Gorget is a wide collar of shells over her throat and shoulders. With a diving dress packed, its helmet hangs at her hip and the canvas suit is rolled on her back until the Belfry, where she wears it; the Plated Dress has shell plates over the chest. She carries her open lantern above the Belfry either way.
- **Agreed:** Wren's control of the water comes from the keeper's craft. Keepers use the Brass Tide-Bell, a hand-bell, to move water in a pool, stair, or cut. The Drowned Bellwether is the same kind of tool, operating across the coast. It is sunk, still ringing, and holding the sea over the Heart.
- **Agreed (2026-11-14), in the game (v0.24.1):** Wren's tide-bell hangs at her belt, and the kit screens point the Bell place there. The lantern stays in her raised hand. A keeper does not put down the lantern or let it leave her hand, because it holds the last flame lit from the Heart. The bell must not take the lantern's hand in any picture.
- **In the game:** The Drowned Bellwether is bronze. It rings, swings its clapper, tolls, and pulls the Swell down. At two thirds of its health (half until v0.16.0), it cracks and calls a shoal of Bell Minnows. It was the Shallows' boss until v0.19.0. Since v0.20.0 it hangs in the Belfry, and it does not simply die (since v0.23.0 its picture is a cracked bronze bell on an iron yoke, with a golden glow and its clapper inside and only a few pale tendrils at the rim; the eye and the arms show only when the Thing comes out): when its bronze breaks, **the Thing in the Bell** comes out, with one eye and many arms, and two Grasping Arms with it. The Thing drinks from Wren's lantern through its arms (Doused 1 for each arm), opens its eye (it is Soaked and takes more damage), glares, and grows its arms back. - **In the game (v0.20.0):** The Shallows' pin is **the Harbour Hermit**: a hermit crab as big as a rowing boat, wearing Saltmere's old harbour bell for a shell. The bell rang the boats home in fog, until a winter storm tore it off the harbour wall. Now it answers the Bellwether and holds the water over the Shallows' stair. It pulls into the bell, and its Ward stays from turn to turn (In its Bell). A Crest rings the bell around its ears and a Strand leaves it stuck on the rock: either shakes it out. Its Bell Crush hits harder each time. Hurt, it rings the harbour bell, and two Barnacle Mites drop off it to help.
- **In the game (v0.22.0):** Once Wren has broken the Hermit's bell, the Workshop fishes it out of the Shallows and mends the crack with brass staples. As kit it is the Harbour Bell: it was armour before it was a bell again, so it guards (3 Ward) on every Crest and Strand and draws nothing. Breaking the Tolling Buoy opens the Toggle Iron, a whaler's harpoon head that turns sideways in the wound.
- **In the game (v0.20.0): the Belfry's creatures.** It is under the sea, around the great bell, where the Heart's light is strongest. **Bell Jellies** are jellyfish shaped like little bells; their chime makes another creature's next move happen twice (Echo). **Bellspawn** are the young of the thing in the bell, one eye and many arms, and they squeeze the air out of the dress (Breathless). **Glimmer Bladders** are light-eaters so full of the Heart's light that they burst when they die (Burst). Its Dreads: the **Drowned Ringers**, keepers who went down to tend the bell and stayed, and who still ring their hand-bells for it (one of them may be the keeper from the diving bell); the **Crowned Oarfish**, the king of herrings, in from the open sea through the broken arch; and the Glimmer Chain from Dark Water. Murk Rays, Lantern Eels, Gloom Jellies, Glimmer Leeches and Bell Minnows are there too.
- **In the game (v0.20.0):** **The Drowned Cantor** leads a choir of drowned keepers under the silent bell, after the Heart is home. Silence the Cantor and the others stop and drift back into the dark.
- **Agreed:** The Bellwether itself waits in the Belfry. Shallower band bosses are pins: ringers that answer the Bellwether. They are separate from the Bellwether and the Heart. Crabs, kelp, eels, gulls, and the drowned are part of the coast's existing life, not pins. A draining chamber strands them.
- **In the game (v0.15.0), agreed:** Dark Water's pin is **the Tolling Buoy**: Saltmere's bell-buoy from before the lighthouse, which rang on its chain off the harbour rocks so ships heard the rocks before they saw them. A storm broke its chain and it sank; now it rings for the Bellwether and holds the water over Dark Water's stair. It still rocks on the water, so the further Wren pushes the Swell from the middle, the harder it swings. Light-eaters live in its cage. Hurt (at two thirds of its health), it snaps its chain and swings free, and then it rings wild: the Wild Peal. - **In the game (v0.15.0), agreed: what eats the light.** In Dark Water the water has been held still for a hundred years, so the brinelight in it has nowhere to go, and things have learned to live on it. This is the central cause made visible: the keepers held the water still, and something grew in it. In the game: the **Glimmer Leech** (heals by what it takes), the **Glimmer Chain** (a siphonophore, a single animal made of many glowing bells, that drinks from Wren's lantern: Doused), and the **Glimmer Garden** (rooted animals whose beads are mouths full of brinelight; a picked bead hardens into a pearl, and something tends the garden and notices what is taken).

## Story so far

Story so far is a screen on the title and in the menu. Its text is in `BL.STORY` in js/data.js, and everything listed in this section is **In the game**. The chapters were rewritten for the three-level expedition in v0.20.0.

- **Story tab:** Eight chapters. Chapter 1 is the opening and is always open. The others open on the first fall, on breaking the Shallows' and Dark Water's pins, and on bringing the Heart home.
  - *The sea gives you back* (first fall): Wren wakes on the shingle below the lighthouse with her lantern at an ember. The bell called back the keepsakes she found.
  - *The harbour bell* (the Shallows' pin): the Harbour Hermit wore Saltmere's old harbour bell. When it cracked, its water ran down an old stair. The Heart was not there; the harbour bell only answers a bigger one. Going home is safe, but the water comes back before the next tide and the next expedition starts from the shore.
  - *Why the bell was sunk* (Dark Water's pin): the bell ringing in Dark Water was Saltmere's old bell-buoy, which held the water over the last stair. The keepers cast the Bellwether and sank it to hold the water off their low stairs and quay. It had to be tended at the lowest tides; the last to go was the lamplighter, a hundred years ago.
  - *The diving dress* (Dark Water's pin): below Dark Water is the Belfry, a bell tower with its bell at the bottom, full of water that does not drain. An old keeper's diving dress with a sealed lamp hung above its stair; Wren carried it home and mended it. Packed in the Coat place, it opens the last stair. It does nothing in a fight.
  - *The Heart comes home* (the Heart): the Bellwether rang over the Heart with no hand on its rope. When its bronze broke, the Heart's light poured out, and with it a thing with one eye and many arms that had grown into the bronze. Wren does not know if she killed it. She carried the Heart up three levels, and the water stopped rising.
  - *Why the fire followed* (the Heart): hand-bells are tuned to the Bellwether and lanterns are lit from the Heart. The tide swings the clapper and the ringing holds the tide, so it rang louder every year. The Heart answered the lead bell.
  - *What stays below* (the Heart): the lighthouse is lit, but the Reach has not gone back. A faint light stayed where the Heart lay, and things gather around it: the thing grows back in the broken bronze, and drowned keepers come to ring the bell. Saltmere still needs a keeper who goes down. It also explains currents.
- **The world tab:** A glossary of places, people and magic. It is always open. v0.20.0 added the Belfry and the diving dress.
- **Keeper's notes tab:** 33 notes, each opened by first meeting its subject. v0.20.0 added the Harbour Hermit, the Thing in the Bell, Bellspawn, Bell Jellies, Glimmer Bladders, the Drowned Ringers, the Crowned Oarfish, the Drowned Choir, the Lost Lanterns, the glow under the bell and the bell-rope. New facts in them: the Heart burns under water as well as in air, and everything in the Belfry grew toward it. A wick lit from the Heart keeps a little of whoever carried it. The keepers once rang the Bellwether by hand, with a rope up the tower. One of the Drowned Ringers may be the keeper from the diving bell.
- **Agreed:** As levels are added, each gets a chapter. New people, places and enemies get a note.
