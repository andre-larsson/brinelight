This file describes the target setting and story for Brinelight. Future game design, cards, events, and screens should follow the agreed details here. Some of them are not in the game yet. Rules and balance are in README.md and GAME_NOTES.md. Card and enemy lists are in js/data.js.

- **Agreed:** A decision for the target world. Where it differs from the current game, the game needs to change to match it.
- **Open:** An unanswered question. When it is answered, record the decision and remove the question from the open list.

## The idea

The keeper's craft has two halves: the light and the bell. Wren plays with both. Light is what she spends on cards, and her bell moves the water. The story is what happens when the great bell takes the great light.

Since v0.62.0 the story also says where the two halves came from. The fire burned under the bell, at the bottom of the cliffs, until the keepers carried it up into a lighthouse a hundred years ago. The bell stayed below, alone. So the great bell does not steal the light: it takes it back. See "Before the keepers, and the year the light went up".

### Before the keepers, and the year the light went up (2026-11-23; the agent's calls, in the game since v0.62.0)

The user asked whether the Tide-Witch makes sense: where she came from, why she is there, whether she has always been there, and whether this is a world where witches are common. The review found that she, the forgotten goddess, the Hollow Siren, the Reefback Hulk, the Angler Twins, the Fog Wisp and Barnaby were left from before the game had a world. It also found contradictions in the dates (GAME_NOTES.md, "Lore review"). The two parts below answer both. All of it is the agent's call, and the user can overrule any of it.

**The timeline.** Later text has to agree with it.

1. **Before the keepers.** Saltmere is a fishing harbour, and it pays the sea for what it needs. It pays in blood at the altar of a sea goddess. It pays in years of a life to the tide-wives, the women who sing the water for the boats. Its fishers leave the first fish of every season on the low rocks for the Hulk.
2. **The keepers.** They tend a fire that burns in a chamber at the bottom of the cliffs, in water as well as in air: the Lantern Heart. Where the fire came from is not said (see the hint under "The goddess" below). They light their lanterns from it. They cast the Bellwether, hang it over the fire and ring it by a rope, and it holds the sea off the low stairs and the quay. Later they let the sea in over it: the bell is sunk, and they tend bell and fire from a diving bell, and later in diving dresses, going down at the lowest tides of the year. Every other bell on the coast is cast to answer the Bellwether: the harbour bell, the bell-buoy and the toll bells. With a bell that holds the sea, nobody needs to pay the sea any more. The altar is forgotten, and the tide-wives' trade dies.
3. **A hundred years ago: the storm, and the lighthouse.** One winter storm takes both of Saltmere's warning bells. It tears the harbour bell off the harbour wall and breaks the bell-buoy's chain. Ships have nothing to hear the rocks by. Saltmere builds the lighthouse on its rock, and the keepers carry the fire up to it, where ships can see it. A keeper called the lamplighter lights the great lamp. Then he goes down once more to tend the bell, and drowns on the low stair, the part of the keepers' stair that is under the sea. Nobody goes down after him, because the keeping is in the tower now.
4. **The hundred years.** The bell rings a little louder every year, and holds a little more water. The two lost bells answer it and become pins. A little of the fire's light stayed in the stone where it had lain, and lost lanterns add to it. The light gathers in the held water as brinelight, and things grow in it.
5. **Tonight.** The bell rings loud enough to be heard in the town. The Heart answers the lead bell and goes back down to the place where it burned before there was a tower.

- **Why this timeline.** It is one turning point, and "a hundred years" now always means the same year. Before v0.62.0 the game's text disagreed with itself:
  - The buoy was lost "before the lighthouse was built", but Hester had heard it and polished its nameplate.
  - The Heart went down tonight, but a note said it had warmed the Belfry for a hundred years, and lost lanterns rolled "toward the fire" under the bell.
  - Keepers were older than the lighthouse (the tide-board), but a keeper was whoever carried a lantern lit from the lighthouse's fire.
  - The tide-board had a cut line for each of the last hundred years, in a place where no keeper had been for a hundred years. (The picture shows cut lines to the top, so the text keeps them and says who cut them: see the tide-board under "Places".)
- **It supports the central cause and does not add a second one.** The keepers changed the Reach twice to make Saltmere safer: they sank a bell to hold the sea, and they carried the fire up to light the rocks. Both worked, and ships came home by the light for a hundred years. Left alone together, the two changes made tonight. The Heart still goes down because a keeper's light answers the lead bell.
- **What the player is told, and when.** The opening is unchanged (the user chose its words). "Tonight the bell rang by itself" stays: it rang quietly for a hundred years, and tonight the town heard it.
  - In the Shallows: the Lost Lamplighter's note says he lit the great lamp the night the lighthouse was finished. Hester's first job brings home the keepers' stair log, and its last line is his: "Lamp lit last night. Gone down to see to the bell."
  - When Dark Water's pin breaks: *Why the bell was sunk* tells the storm, the lighthouse and the fire carried up.
  - In the Belfry: the note on the glow under the bell says the Heart burned there before there was a lighthouse.
  - When the Heart is home: *Why the fire followed* says the keepers parted the light and the bell, and that the fire went back.
- **Hints, not facts.** The Drowned Cantor's choir sings "you took the Heart up the stair to the town, but we were told to stay". Wren is the second keeper to carry the Heart up that stair. Who told the ringers to stay, a hundred years ago, is still open.

**The old bargain.** This is the answer to "is this a world where witches are common?". It is not. Old Saltmere had one way of living with the sea, and three things are left of it.

- **The idea.** Before the keepers, the sea was paid, in person and at once: blood, years, the first fish. The keepers' light and bell ended the paying, and that was a real good. The cost of the bell came later, to everybody. The two Shallows events already play this way: the Shrine takes HP and the Tide-Witch takes Max HP. Game text must not say that the old way was kinder. It was not.
- **The Tide-Witch** is the last tide-wife. The tide-wives sang the water for Saltmere's boats, for a price: a slack tide to cross the bar, a fair wind tied into a knot. "Tide-Witch" is the town's word for the one who is left. Her hut stands on stilts below the tide line in the Shallows, where the town's ground ends, and the town does not come down there. There has been a crone in the hut for as long as Saltmere remembers, and nobody likes to ask whether it has always been the same one. Her lessons are the old craft, older than the keepers' book. Her price is years off a life, which she knots into her shawl of net. Her brews are the tonics, and the tonics found in the Reach are bottles she sold to sailors, kept by the held water. The held water also remembers them (v0.63.0): it keeps everything it takes, and brinelight copies, so an empty flask filled where brinelight stands deep, on a stair down or under a lesser bell, is one of her brews again. That is how filling a flask "with brinelight" gives the same tonics, and why the bottle in the Fog can turn out to be one. An apprentice is possible (an old keeper idea, "the Tide-Witch's apprentice, who wants the bell and not the Heart").
- **The goddess** of the Sunken Shrine is whom old Saltmere asked and paid. Her name is forgotten because nobody has needed to ask since the bell. Her hands are empty because nobody brings an offering.
  - **A hint, not a fact:** the water at her altar turns warm when she takes an offering, as water does wherever the Heart's light gathers. Hester says it is only brinelight. The note says the old town had another name for it, "and that is the name nobody remembers". The agent's reading is that the goddess and the fire may be one thing, seen by the old town and by the keepers. The game does not say so, and it stays open with the ending. Do not settle it without the user.
- **The Hollow Siren** sings the tide-wives' slack-water note, the one they sold to the fishing boats. Nobody is singing it. The held water kept the note and grew a shape around it, as a pearl grows around grit, and the shape is the one sailors always said was out there. "Hollow" is the keepers' word for a shape with nobody in it, as with the Hollow Divers. The bell rings with no hand on its rope, and the note sounds with no singer.
- **The Reefback Hulk** is what the fishers fed. It slept under the low rocks for years at a time, and a reef grew on its back. The held water has kept it wet and awake for a hundred years. What it is under the reef is not said.

### Environmental theme and cosmic horror (2026-11-12; agreed, not built)

- **Agreed:** The underlying story is that the keepers changed the Reach to make Saltmere safer, and something has grown in the conditions they created. This is the central cause; other environmental ideas should support it without becoming several separate explanations for the crisis.
- **Agreed:** The magical coast is a metaphor for human impact on the environment. Human progress can save lives and support livelihoods while also causing unintended, accumulating harm. The lighthouse and the keepers' tools have real benefits. Responsibility can differ between people who could not foresee the effects and people who later understood them.
- **Agreed:** Keep the environmental meaning underneath the adventure. Show changes through places, creatures, objects and keeper records. The opening still gives Wren a concrete task: the light is gone, the water is rising, and she must bring the Heart home. Avoid moral speeches and a simple division into good nature and bad humans.
- **Agreed:** Cosmic horror comes from the scale and nature of what has taken hold around human work. Wren can learn how to handle particular dangers while remaining unsure what the larger thing is or what it perceives. Human activity has consequences even when the thing responding has no human intention to punish anyone.
- **Agreed:** The ending is ambiguous about what survives and what the Reach is becoming. Wren's immediate success is clear: she recovers the Heart and saves Saltmere. Useful knowledge, better equipment and adaptation can improve life afterward, even where changes to the coast last.
- **Open:** The nature of what has grown around the bell, and how much Wren can discover about it. The exact final scene is also open. A faint light remaining in the Belfry after the Heart is removed is a candidate image, not a fixed ending.

## The situation

Wren is the last lighthouse keeper of Saltmere. The lighthouse went dark on her watch. Far below, the Drowned Bellwether rang with no hand on its rope, and the Lantern Heart went down to it. Wren goes down the coast to bring it back.

Since v0.20.0 the game tells this story in three levels: the Shallows, Dark Water and the Belfry, where the Bellwether hangs over the Heart. The held water has those bells now, and like every lost bell on this coast they answer the Bellwether. ## Why they go down

- **Agreed:** Wren goes because the light went out on her watch, and because hers is the last flame lit from the Heart.
- **Agreed:** Saltmere needs her to go. The ordinary sea gives back what it takes. Held water gives nothing back, so the wrecks, the drowned, and the Heart stay down. Each spring tide the held water stands a stair closer to the town. The game text says it more simply: the bell lifts the sea toward the town, a little higher with every tide. The rules have no countdown for this.
- **Agreed:** Before the Heart comes home, everything Wren opens closes behind her because the Bellwether restores the water after each expedition. Reaching the final depth and silencing it ends the immediate threat to Saltmere. The altered Reach remains after that victory; see "After the Heart comes home" below.
- **Agreed:** Carrying a lantern lit from the Heart is what makes a keeper. Wren is the last keeper when the game starts. Later playable characters become keepers when they light their lamps at the Heart, once it is home (2026-11-16; before, Wren was to light them from her lantern). - **Agreed (2026-11-16, the user's call; in the game for Mateo since v0.53.0): how a new keeper is lit.**

  - Each new keeper brings a lamp of their own trade: Josiah's pilot's lamp, a lamp from Abena's forge, Old Lan's ferry lantern. Lit at the Heart, it carries the fire that is the Light they spend on cards. An unlit lamp gives nothing to fight with below.

  - **The stranger's Dread.** Grief and fear in Saltmere run down into the sea too, not only what a keeper carries. ## The expedition

Every expedition starts on shore in the Shallows and can continue through every band Wren is willing to risk. Wren packs once on shore and carries the same pack throughout. Starting a new expedition at a deeper band is not possible.

Story so far is a screen on the title and in the menu. Its text is in `BL.STORY` in js/data.js, and everything listed in this section is **In the game**. The chapters were rewritten for the three-level expedition in v0.20.0.

- **Story tab:** Twelve chapters (ten until Mateo's two in v0.53.0). Chapter 1 is the opening and is always open. The others open on the first fall, on breaking the Shallows' and Dark Water's pins, on first going down into the Belfry, on bringing the Heart home, and on first beating the Dark Watch.
  - *The sea gives you back* (first fall): Wren wakes on the shingle below the lighthouse with her lantern at an ember. Since v0.43.0: Bosun went in after her, swam her in by her scarf and barked until Hester came down with a blanket. The bell called back the keepsakes she found.
  - *The harbour bell* (the Shallows' pin): the Harbour Hermit wore Saltmere's old harbour bell. When it cracked, its water ran down an old stair. The Heart was not there; the harbour bell only answers a bigger one. Going home is safe, but the water comes back before the next tide and the next expedition starts from the shore.
  - *Why the bell was sunk* (Dark Water's pin): the bell ringing in Dark Water was Saltmere's old bell-buoy, which held the water over the last stair. The keepers cast the Bellwether and sank it to hold the water off their low stairs and quay. It had to be tended at the lowest tides; the last to go was the lamplighter, a hundred years ago. Since v0.62.0 it has four paragraphs and also tells the year the light went up: the fire was kept under the bell, one storm took both of the town's bells, Saltmere built the lighthouse and the keepers carried the fire up, and the lamplighter who lit the great lamp was the last to go down.
  - *The diving dress* (Dark Water's pin): below Dark Water is the Belfry, a bell tower with its bell at the bottom, full of water that does not drain. An old keeper's diving dress with a sealed lamp hung above its stair; Wren carried it home and mended it. Packed in the Coat place, it opens the last stair. It does nothing in a fight.
  - *Into the Belfry* (v0.40.0, first time down into the Belfry): Wren seals the helmet on the last dry step, lights its lamp from her lantern, leaves the open lantern on the step and walks down the keepers' stair into the sea. The Belfry is full of bells, and why (the bell rule below). Whatever lives there has learned it too: its young wear broken bronze for shells.
  - *The Heart comes home* (the Heart): the Bellwether rang over the Heart with no hand on its rope. When its bronze broke, the Heart's light poured out, and with it a thing with one eye and many arms that had grown into the bronze. Wren does not know if she killed it. She carried the Heart up three levels, and the water stopped rising.
  - *Why the fire followed* (the Heart): hand-bells are tuned to the Bellwether and lanterns are lit from the Heart. The tide swings the clapper and the ringing holds the tide, so it rang louder every year. The Heart answered the lead bell. Since v0.62.0: the keepers parted the two, ships came home by the light for a hundred years while the bell stayed below alone, and the Heart went back to the place where it burned before there was a tower.
  - *What stays below* (the Heart): the lighthouse is lit, but the Reach has not gone back. Something still answers in the pins, the water still comes back over the landings, and new things fill the caves. Brinelight grows toward the brightest light near it: for a hundred years that was the glow the Heart left under the bell, and now it is the lantern of whoever goes down, so what grows below takes its shape from what the keeper brings (grief, regret, fear). Saltmere still needs a keeper who goes down. It also explains currents. (An earlier text had the thing growing back in the broken bronze. The chapter does not say that: what became of the Thing stays unknown.)
- **Story scenes** (v0.33.0, **In the game**): six chapters also play as pictures, once, where they happen (going down into the Belfry since v0.40.0; the Dark Watch since v0.37.0). Later text has to agree with them:
  - *The Harbour Hermit breaks:* the harbour bell cracks from rim to crown; the crab lets go of the water it held and runs (it is not killed); the keepers' stair comes up out of the sea after a hundred years under water. The Heart is not there, and its glow goes on down the stair.
  - *The Tolling Buoy breaks:* the buoy rang off Saltmere's rocks before there was a lighthouse. Its water runs down the last stair and does not drain at the bottom. The keepers' name for the chamber below is the Belfry. An open lantern cannot go there. The diving dress hangs on the wall above the last dry step, its seams rotted and its lamp dry.
  - *The dress is mended:* Wren mends it herself, in the boathouse on the harbour, with waxed thread and lamp oil, and tries the sealed lamp with the glass shut. Its picture (`sc_mend`) has her alone at the bench under a lamp hung from a beam, with the dark lighthouse across the water. So since v0.63.0 the text says that Hester hangs the lamp over the bench (it said she held it), and the homecoming screen says the dress is mended "in the boathouse on the harbour" (it said "at the lighthouse").
  - *Into the Belfry* (v0.40.0): the open lantern cannot go under the sea, so it stays on the last dry step, lit; the helmet's sealed lamp is lit from it. Pictures: Wren in the dress wading off the last step (`sc_belfry1`), a drowned tower full of lost bells, a tipped bell-buoy cage, hand-bells on ropes and bell-shaped jellies, with the great bell's glow far below (`sc_belfry2`), and the Bellwether's chamber (`bg_belfry_boss`).
  - *The Heart comes home:* the Thing lets go of the Heart and sinks into the dark under the Belfry’s floor. The water runs away in front of the Heart as Wren carries it up. By dawn the great light turns, Hester and Bosun watch from the path below the tower, and the boats go out past the rocks.
- **What the player is told about the levels** (v0.33.0): a level is not named anywhere in the game until Wren has reached it or broken the pin above it. Text a new player can see must not name Dark Water before the Hermit breaks, or the Belfry before the Buoy does.   - **Agreed (2026-11-23, the user's rule; in the game since v0.63.0): an entry's text never changes.** The user: "I dont want to change texts in glossary throughout progress, only unlock new ones."
